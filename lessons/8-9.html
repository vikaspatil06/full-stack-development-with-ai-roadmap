1. Monitoring answers: is it up, and is it healthy?

▶ YouTube reference — the video for this lesson

Uptime checks ping your service from outside and tell you when it is down — ideally before a learner does. Log alerts go further: they watch for patterns, like a spike in 500 errors or a sudden rise in Gemini failures, and notify you. The difference matters: a service can be up and still broken, answering every request with an error page. Skillzify's staging needs both — a health endpoint the checker pings, and an alert on error rate — because the tutor failing silently is worse than the site being down, since nobody reports what they assume is their own mistake.

2. Analytics funnels find the broken step

▶ YouTube reference — the video for this lesson

A funnel is a sequence of steps with a conversion rate at each one: visited a lesson, started it, finished it, earned XP. Where the funnel narrows sharply, something is broken — a confusing exercise, a slow page, a tutor that fails on one topic. GA4 builds these from events, so the events you emit decide what you can learn: a lesson_complete event with the lesson ID is worth ten pageview counts. Read funnels weekly, not daily — small numbers are noise, and a single day's dip is usually a timetable, not a bug.

3. The incident runbook

▶ YouTube reference — the video for this lesson

When something breaks at midnight, you should not be inventing a process. A runbook lists the symptoms, the first three checks, the rollback step, and who to tell. For Skillzify it reads: check Render status and logs, check the Gemini key and quota, check Firestore rules and status, then roll back the last deploy if the breakage started with it. Write it while things work, test it during a real incident, and update it after — a runbook that has never been used is a draft, not a plan.

Monitoring is not about dashboards — it is about the time between something breaking and someone who can fix it knowing.

Why is an uptime check alone not enough monitoring?

A service can be up and still broken — returning errors on every request. You also need error-rate alerts and health checks that verify real behaviour.

What makes a funnel useful, and what makes it noise?

Useful when each step is a real user action and the sample is large; noise when numbers are tiny or steps are pageviews instead of meaningful events.

7 · Interview ▾

Your error rate spikes at 2 AM. What is your first move?

Follow the runbook: check deploy recency and logs, identify whether it started with a release, and roll back first — diagnose after users are unblocked.

How do you choose what to alert on versus what to dashboard?

Alert on things needing a human now — downtime, error spikes. Dashboard the rest — trends you review weekly, like funnel conversion.
