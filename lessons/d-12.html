Goal — by the end of this lessonBy the end you can run a full 45-minute mock interview round, diagnose your own failures with a mistake log, and walk into a Summer 2027 internship interview with a practiced pattern-first routine.

1. The 45-minute anatomy of an interview round

▶ YouTube reference — the video for this lesson

A real round has a rhythm: five minutes clarifying the problem and confirming constraints, ten minutes on a brute force with its complexity, fifteen minutes optimising to the target pattern, ten minutes coding cleanly, and five minutes testing. The most common failure is coding in the first five minutes — it skips the thinking the interviewer is actually grading. Practice the rhythm with a timer, not just the problems. If you cannot state the brute force and its complexity before coding, you are not ready to code.

2. Thinking aloud and the pattern-first reflex

▶ YouTube reference — the video for this lesson

Interviewers grade your thinking, not just your code, so narrate the recognition step: what pattern does this resemble, and why? Pair sums mean two pointers or a hash map; substrings mean sliding window; trees mean recursion; shortest paths mean BFS; optimisation over choices means DP. Build the reflex by naming the pattern before writing a line — in practice and in mocks. When you are stuck, say so out loud and name what you have tried; silence reads as giving up, while a stated dead end reads as problem-solving.

3. The mistake log and spaced re-solve

▶ YouTube reference — the video for this lesson

Every failed or 45-plus-minute problem goes in the log with three lines: the pattern, what went wrong, and a re-solve date. Re-solve after 3 days, then after 7 days, without looking at the old solution — if you cannot reproduce it, the pattern is not learned and the cycle repeats. The log is also your revision list for the final week: problems you failed twice are the ones most likely to appear in some form, and re-solving them cold is the highest-value revision there is.

4. Contests, mocks, and the final two weeks

▶ YouTube reference — the video for this lesson

Do the LeetCode Weekly Contest every Sunday: four problems under real time pressure, with no pausing and no editorials until the timer ends. Pair it with one full mock per week — a friend or a recorded self-interview, 45 minutes, talking aloud. In the final two weeks, stop learning new patterns; re-solve your mistake log, redo the core five per pattern, and rest the day before the interview. One rest day per week throughout the plan is not laziness — it is when the patterns consolidate.

def two_sum(nums, target):
    seen = {}
    for i, x in enumerate(nums):
        need = target - x
        if need in seen:
            return [seen[need], i]
        seen[x] = i
    return []

assert two_sum([2, 7, 11, 15], 9) == [0, 1]
assert two_sum([3, 3], 6) == [0, 1]

What is the five-part structure of a 45-minute interview round?

Clarify (5 min), brute force with complexity (10), optimise to the pattern (15), code cleanly (10), test with edge cases (5).

How does the spaced re-solve rule work?

Re-solve a failed problem after 3 days, then after 7 days, without looking at the old solution. If you cannot reproduce it, the cycle repeats.

7 · Interview ▾

You are stuck 15 minutes into a problem. What do you do?

Say so out loud, restate what the pattern should be, try the brute force to buy a working solution, and ask the interviewer a clarifying question — silence is the only wrong move.

How do you test your solution before telling the interviewer you are done?

Walk through the empty input, a single element, duplicates, and the boundary case, then trace one small example by hand and check the complexity aloud.
