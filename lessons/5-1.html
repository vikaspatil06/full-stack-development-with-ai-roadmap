1. A model predicts the next token. That's the whole trick.

▶ YouTube reference — the video for this lesson

An LLM reads text as tokens — chunks of roughly 3–4 characters — and predicts the most plausible next token, again and again. Everything else (chat, code, tutoring) is scaffolding around that loop. Consequences you must internalise:

  • Cost and limits are counted in tokens — input plus output. A 50,000-character paste is a budget event.
  • The model has no memory between calls. "Conversation memory" is your app re-sending the history each time.
  • It predicts plausible text, not verified facts — hence hallucinations: fluent, confident, wrong. Mitigation is architectural (grounding, retrieval, citations — Lesson 5.5), not "prompt harder".

2. The context window

▶ YouTube reference — the video for this lesson

Everything the model can consider in one call — system prompt, history, your question, retrieved documents — must fit a fixed token window. Long inputs push earlier content out or get rejected. Designing what earns a place in the window is AI engineering.

3. Temperature and friends

▶ YouTube reference — the video for this lesson

Temperature controls sampling randomness: low (≈0–0.3) for factual, repeatable tasks like grading or JSON output; higher (≈0.7–1.0) for brainstorming and drills variety. Skillzify's modes differ here deliberately — an error explainer should be stable; a quiz generator benefits from variety.

The system prompt is your productSame model, different system prompt = different product. Skillzify's tutor persona, its Socratic hinting rules and its output formats all live in prompts maintained in server.js — treat them as versioned product code, not strings you tweak casually.

Why does an LLM hallucinate, in one honest sentence?

It generates statistically plausible text, not retrieved facts — when knowledge runs out, plausible-sounding invention is the natural continuation.

Your chat app 'remembers' yesterday's conversation. What's really happening?

The app stores the history and re-sends it (or a summary) inside the context window on each new call; the model itself is stateless.

6 · Interview

Explain tokens and context windows to a non-technical founder.

Tokens are the word-chunks the model reads and bills by; the context window is its desk size — everything it can look at for one answer must fit on the desk.

How do you reduce hallucinations in a production feature?

Ground answers in retrieved sources, constrain output formats, use low temperature for factual tasks, show citations, and add evals that measure error rates per release.
